\subsubsection{Stranded Mass}
By default the mobile domain scales the solute mass of a cell by the saturation of the cell, so when the water table falls the solute that the drained part of the cell held is returned to the water that remains and the simulated concentration rises.  Solute that stays behind when a cell drains does not move with the water table, and the STRANDED\_MASS option holds that mass out of the mobile domain until the cell rewets.

Two quantities are held back.  The first is the solute in the water that does not drain.  The volume of water in the mobile domain changes by the porosity times the change in saturation, but the flow model releases only the drainable part of the pore space to storage, and the difference between the two is the water that is retained against drainage.  That volume is not specified by the user; it follows from the POROSITY of this package and the specific yield of the Storage (STO) Package of the flow model, and it is zero when the two are equal.  The second is the sorbed mass, because the solid aquifer material does not drain.  The option therefore has an effect whether or not sorption is active; without sorption only the solute in the retained water is held back.  Stranded mass is not transported and takes no part in dispersion.

When a cell rewets, the stranded mass is assumed to be distributed uniformly through the part of the cell that drained while the mass was held, and the share of that part which rewets is returned to the mobile domain.  The mass that is returned is the mass that was held out of the mobile domain by the same cell, so no mass is created or destroyed and no neighboring cell is used.  A cell that drains and then rewets completely recovers all of the mass it gave up; a cell that rewets part of the way recovers only the corresponding share, because the mass in the part that stays dry stays there.

Stranded mass decays at the rates given by DECAY and DECAY\_SORBED, so that solute in each phase decays at the rate that belongs to it.  Decay of stranded mass never passes through the concentration equation, so it is reported in a separate budget for the package rather than in the model budget; only the transfer between the mobile domain and the stranded mass, which is reported as STORAGE-STRANDED, appears in the model budget.  Stranded mass does not decay when zero-order decay is active, because zero-order decay would drive an empty reservoir negative.

The saturation of a cell at the end of the previous time step is needed to determine how much of the cell drained, and it is not available during the first time step of a simulation.  That time step is treated as it would be without the option, and no mass is stranded during it.  A simulation that begins with a large change in the water table should include a short leading stress period during which the water table does not move.

The STRANDED\_MASS option cannot be used together with the Immobile Storage and Transfer (IST) Package.  An immobile domain also scales its mass by the saturation of the cell, so it would return its solute to the mobile domain on drainage while the mobile domain held its own mass out, and the two domains would be inconsistent with one another.  The option should also not be used together with the Unsaturated Zone Transport (UZT) Package, which represents the drained part of a cell explicitly.
