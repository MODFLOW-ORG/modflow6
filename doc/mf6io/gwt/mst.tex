\subsubsection{Stranded Mass}
By default the mobile domain scales both the dissolved and the sorbed solute mass by the saturation of the cell, so when a cell drains the solute that the cell held is returned to the water that remains and the simulated concentration rises.  Sorbed solute does not move with the water table, because the solid phase does not drain, and the STRANDED\_MASS option holds that mass out of the mobile domain until the cell rewets.

Stranded mass applies to sorbed mass, so the STRANDED\_MASS option has no effect unless the SORPTION keyword is also specified, and a warning is issued if it is used without sorption.  Stranded mass is not transported and takes no part in dispersion.

When a cell rewets, the stranded mass is assumed to be distributed uniformly through the part of the cell that drained while the mass was held, and the share of that part which rewets is returned to the mobile domain.  The mass that is returned is the mass that was held out of the mobile domain by the same cell, so no mass is created or destroyed and no neighboring cell is used.  A cell that drains and then rewets completely recovers all of the mass it gave up; a cell that rewets part of the way recovers only the corresponding share, because the mass in the part that stays dry stays there.

Stranded mass decays at the rate given by DECAY\_SORBED.  Decay of stranded mass never passes through the concentration equation, so it is reported in a separate budget for the package rather than in the model budget; only the transfer between the mobile domain and the stranded mass, which is reported as STORAGE-STRANDED, appears in the model budget.  Stranded mass does not decay when zero-order decay is active, because zero-order decay would drive an empty reservoir negative.

Solute held by water that does not drain is not represented.  Only the sorbed phase is stranded.

The STRANDED\_MASS option cannot be used together with the Immobile Storage and Transfer (IST) Package.  An immobile domain also scales its mass by the saturation of the cell, so it would return its solute to the mobile domain on drainage while the mobile domain held its own mass out, and the two domains would be inconsistent with one another.
